# Form Submissions Dropped After Redesign? Here is Why

**By Moksh, Founder of Rankur**

You just launched your brand-new website. The design looks slick, the brand agency praised the modern aesthetics, and the board approved the new visuals.

Then, week two after launch hits... and inbound form submissions plummet by 50%.

This is every founder's nightmare: **you spent $50,000+ on a shiny new website, only to watch your sales pipeline collapse**.

Why do website redesigns so frequently crush lead generation? Because most traditional design agencies prioritize visual aesthetics and trendy animations over **conversion rate engineering, page speed, and technical SEO architecture**.

Here is why your form submissions dropped after your redesign, and the step-by-step technical audit to restore your inbound lead volume.

---

## 1. You Traded Clear Messaging for Trendy Design

Traditional web design agencies love minimalist design trends: subtle grey text on white backgrounds, hidden navigation menus, and abstract headline copy.

While this looks great in an agency design portfolio, it confuses real enterprise buyers.

When a prospect lands on your redesigned homepage, if they cannot figure out within 3 seconds what you do and how to buy, they leave.

### The Fix: Re-establish Clarity Above the Fold
- **Bold, High-Contrast Headlines:** Ensure text is hyper-readable (contrast ratio > 4.5:1).
- **Explicit Value Proposition:** Replace abstract phrases (*"We craft digital futures"*) with concrete business outcomes (*"Custom B2B web design live in 7 days"*).
- **Prominent CTAs:** Make primary conversion buttons high-contrast and instantly visible without scrolling.

---

## 2. Broken Mobile Form UX and Layout Shifts

Designers usually present website mockups on desktop monitors. But over half of your B2B buyers evaluate your site on mobile devices.

Post-redesign lead drops are frequently caused by mobile UX failures:
* Sticky headers or cookie banners covering the contact form submit button.
* Form fields failing to open the correct mobile keyboard (e.g. numeric keyboard for phone numbers).
* Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) causing the form to jump around while loading, leading to accidental misclicks.

---

## 3. Missing 301 Redirects and SEO Traffic Losses

If your redesign involved changing URL structures (e.g. moving from `/services/b2b-web-design` to `/our-work/web-design`) without setting up proper **301 redirects**, Google drops your rankings overnight.

When high-intent search pages disappear from Google, high-intent traffic vanishes—and form submissions drop accordingly.

### Post-Redesign SEO Recovery Protocol
1. Audit your Google Search Console coverage report for 404 errors.
2. Ensure every legacy URL has an explicit 1-to-1 301 redirect mapped to its new counterpart.
3. Resubmit your updated XML sitemap (`sitemap.xml`) to Google Search Console.

---

## 4. Heavy Third-Party Script Bloat Slowing Down Forms

Designers often add heavy third-party plugins (smooth scrolling, custom cursors, background video loops, chat popups) during a redesign.

These scripts block the browser's main thread. A user clicks *"Submit"*, but nothing happens for 3 seconds because the browser is busy processing background JavaScript. Impatient buyers assume the form is broken and close the tab.

### Why do inbound form submissions drop after a website redesign?
Form submissions drop after a redesign due to 4 common failures: sacrificing clear messaging for trendy aesthetics, mobile UX bugs (layout shifts or blocked submit buttons), missing 301 redirects causing organic traffic loss, and heavy JavaScript scripts delaying form responsiveness.

### How do you fix lead conversion drop-offs after launching a new site?
To fix post-redesign lead drop-offs:
1. Conduct a full mobile usability audit on contact forms across iOS and Android devices.
2. Run a 301 redirect audit to fix 404 errors in Google Search Console.
3. Optimize page performance to ensure Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) is under 2.5 seconds.
4. Replace vague agency headlines with explicit ROI messaging above the fold.
